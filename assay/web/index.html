What Assay can and cannot prove

The audit

Assay is an agent, not a script. It reads your balance and the model catalog, picks the cheapest viable model from several vendors, proves each one can answer, and spends a few cents on fixed public prompts. It compares what the gateway says with what your balance does. If something looks wrong it re-runs a larger sample before calling it a failure.

Money is computed in exact decimals, never floating point. A charge lands after the answer it pays for, so Assay waits for the balance to settle before comparing.

The score, fineness, is purity in parts per thousand, as on a hallmark. Checks that could not run are excluded and shown as lower coverage, so a clean result on little evidence never looks like a strong one.

The benchmark

Bench replays your own prompts against several models through the same key, and grades each answer. Hard checks (valid JSON, schema, exact fields) are deterministic. For fuzzy answers a judge model compares each candidate with your current model twice, with the order swapped, and never sits in the same vendor family as either.

It recommends a switch only when the pessimistic end of a 95% interval on the quality difference stays inside the margin you choose. Otherwise it says how many more prompts would settle the question. Change the margin on the Bench page and the answer re-decides live.

What it cannot tell you

  • Whether prompts are stored. That is invisible from outside; Orbio's claim stands or falls on trust.
  • Which model weights actually ran. It detects mislabelling and gross substitution, not a careful imitation.
  • Whether other clients on your key were active during the audit. Use a dedicated key.
  • Added latency, unless you supply a direct baseline key. Without one, it reports the gateway round-trip only.
  • How a model behaves on prompts you did not test. A benchmark is only as representative as the sample you give it; include your hard cases.
  • Whether an LLM judge agrees with you. Judges have biases; Bench reports how often the judge changed its mind when the order flipped.
